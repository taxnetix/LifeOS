What it is

It's a folder on your computer

There's no website to log into and no database. Everything sits in plain files on your own machine, so you can still open it in ten years on a laptop with no internet. You drop a document in a folder, and LifeOS does the rest.

It checks in on its own

You don't have to remember to run it. It spots a policy about to expire or a deadline coming up, not just new files. When nothing has changed, it says so and stops.

Every number shows its source

Take any figure in any report and you can see the document it came from, down to the page. When LifeOS isn't confident it read something properly, it asks you rather than guessing.

Your details stay on your computer

A built-in guard blocks anything going out that contains your ID number, an account number or a name from your profile. The guard is code, so it still works when the AI gets something wrong.

It admits what it doesn't know

Anything missing gets written down as a gap and kept on a list. You won't find an invented figure, or a zero sitting where nobody actually checked.

It reports its own bugs

When a bank changes its statement layout or a tax table goes out of date, LifeOS raises an issue about itself on GitHub. Those issues never mention anything about you.

You can add your own areas

One command sets up a new area, like a side business or a rental property. LifeOS starts covering it on the next run.

Meet the agents

A specialist for each part of your life

An agent is simply a set of written instructions for one job. Each one says what it looks after, what it stays out of, and when it runs. Six of them keep the system going. The other eleven each take one part of your life.

The machinery

six agents that run the system, not your data

orchestratorthe loop

Runs each cycle and decides what gets done next. It's the only one allowed to hand work to the others, so nothing runs behind its back.

librarianfiling

Sorts your inbox. Files each document, names it, spots duplicates and passes it to the right agent. It never changes or deletes an original.

memory-keepermemory

Remembers what LifeOS has learned about you and drops what's gone stale. When two things contradict each other, it shows you both instead of picking one.

meta-architectthe system

Looks after LifeOS itself: new areas, tidy-ups, bug reports. It can't see your documents at all, which is why it's the one allowed to post in public.

analystthe sums

Does the maths for everyone else: totals, trends, projections, what-ifs. It keeps no records of its own and shows its working every time.

visualiserreports

Builds the dashboards and reports. Each one is a single file that opens in any browser, with no internet needed. It never works out a number itself.

Your life

eleven agents, each in charge of one area

identitypeople

Everyone in the picture: you, your spouse, children, dependants and your companies, plus how they all connect. Everything else hangs off this one.

financemoney

Bank accounts, transactions, debit orders, budgets. Turns a pile of statements into a sorted picture of your spending and a dashboard you can actually read.

livinghealth & home

Medical aid and gap cover, work benefits, subscriptions, online accounts, leases, and who holds your spare keys.

insurancecover

Every policy you hold: life, disability, income protection, dread disease, funeral, car and home. Finds where you're covered twice, and where you're not covered at all.

investmentssavings

Retirement annuities, pension and provident funds, unit trusts, shares, offshore, tax-free savings and crypto. Keeps an eye on what the fees are costing you.

assetswhat you own

Property, cars, valuables, and everything you owe. Including any surety you've signed, which is the one people forget and the one that hurts most.

taxSARS

Deadlines, medical credits, how much more you can still put into retirement, tax-free savings limits. It gets you ready. It doesn't file anything.

estateyour will

Your will, who inherits what, and what winding up the estate will cost. Also checks whether the beneficiaries on your policies match what your will says.

trustsadd-on

Trustees, resolutions, beneficiaries, loan accounts and the yearly section 7C bill. Optional. Add it only if you have a trust.

final-wishesfirst days

Burial or cremation, the plot, funeral contacts, and how your family gets hold of cash in the first month. Kept separate so it's quick to find.

readinessthe Life File

Scores how ready your paperwork is, tells you what's missing and where the originals are kept, then writes the Life File.

Only one agent can change each record.

The rest can read it, but not touch it. A built-in test checks this every single time and fails if two agents ever end up in charge of the same thing. It's what stops them quietly overwriting each other's work.

The main thing it makes

Life File

The document you hand your family

One document with everything your family needs on the worst day of their lives. It comes in three versions, depending on who's reading. None of them ever contains a password, a PIN or a safe code. They only say where to find them.

First 48 Hours

For whoever finds it first. Who to phone, your funeral wishes, where the will is, and what cash your family can actually get to. No ID or account numbers anywhere in it.

Executor Pack

For your executor, spouse or attorney. Policies to claim, accounts, what you own, what you owe, and your online accounts. Numbers show the last four digits only.

Sealed Annexure

Full numbers, for the executor alone. You have to ask for it by name, it comes watermarked, and every copy made gets logged.

The first section is “What my family will not find.”

The will you never got signed. The title deed nobody can put their hands on. The surety you signed for a friend's business. Listing only the things that are sorted would give your family false comfort, so LifeOS leads with the gaps, while you can still fix them.

Have a look at a real one. Both files came straight out of LifeOS, built from a test vault. Every name, policy and number in them is made up.

What it finds

Things you'd never spot in one document

Every one of these came from reading two or more documents together. The numbers below are from a test vault, not a real person.

The estate is worth R5.9m. The family can get R30 000 in the first month.

That's about three weeks of normal household spending. Bank accounts freeze when you die, joint accounts included. Group life takes weeks to pay out. Anything going through the estate takes months. So a family can be well provided for on paper and still struggle to buy groceries.

You're paying for R55 000 a month of income protection you could never claim.

Your work cover and your own policy together add up to more than insurers will actually pay out, so up to R15 408 a year of premiums is buying nothing. Worth trimming your own policy rather than cancelling it, since the work cover disappears the day you leave the job.

R1.8m is going to someone your will doesn't mention.

When you name a beneficiary on a policy or a retirement fund, that beats your will for that money. Neither document mentions the other, so nobody spots it. Often it's exactly what you wanted. It should still be a choice you made on purpose.

Lending your family trust money interest-free costs R53 500 a year.

SARS treats the interest you're not charging as a donation, and you pay the tax personally, in cash, every year the loan stays interest-free. Nothing on the loan account tells you that.

The same subscription on two cards Premiums creeping up Fees you can't see Cover already signed over to the bank Cover that ends with your job Day-to-day medical gaps What fees cost over 30 years No independent trustee No record of how to cancel

Where it hands over

Where LifeOS stops, IntelliTax starts

LifeOS never submits anything to SARS. It gets your paperwork in order, works out the numbers and tracks the deadlines, then tells you when you need a registered practitioner. IntelliTax is where that part happens: a South African tax platform for SARS directives, tax certificates, Recognition of Transfer and employer reconciliation.

GraphQL API

One endpoint covering the whole platform: taxpayers, funds, employers, directives, certificates and billing. For calling from your own code.

MCP server

The same functions offered as tools an AI assistant can use directly. You set it up in a config file instead of writing code against it.

Agent-to-agent

IntelliTax has its own agents that other agents can talk to, so filing becomes one step inside a bigger job instead of a site someone logs into.

This connection isn't built yet.

LifeOS includes notes on how it would work and what you'd need: an IntelliTax account, a subscription, billing and an API key. There is no IntelliTax code inside LifeOS today. If it's ever added, you'd still approve every submission yourself, and the key would live in your settings, never in your vault.

How to start

Three steps, about ten minutes

Free to use and free to change. Your documents stay in a folder of their own that never gets uploaded anywhere, and there's a safety check that stops you sharing them by accident.

  1. Install Claude Code

    Claude Code is Anthropic's app for letting Claude read and write files on your own computer. It's a different thing to the Claude website or the phone app, and it's what LifeOS runs inside.

    Get Claude Code →

  2. Download the LifeOS folder

    Unzip it, then open that folder in Claude Code. Everything LifeOS needs is already inside it, so there's nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

    Download ZIP

  3. Type /lifeos-init

    Words starting with a slash are commands. You type them into Claude Code the same way you'd type a message, then press enter. Typing just / shows you the whole list.

    This first one asks you some questions and sets your folder up. After that, put a bank statement in inbox and type /heartbeat.

Optional

If you're comfortable with a terminal

Cloning instead of downloading lets you add the extra tools that read PDFs and turn the Life File into a printable document. Everything else works fine without them. 

git clone https://github.com/taxnetix/LifeOS.git
cd LifeOS

python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
npm install
bash tools/scripts/install-hooks.sh   # stops your documents being shared

# then, in Claude Code
/lifeos-init    # set up your folder
/heartbeat      # one pass, safe to run any time
